# Reading their files

Step 2 of the interview. Read every file they drop in **before** asking anything, so you can confirm
instead of ask. Open every page properly; zoom on small text rather than guess. Never copy a
customer's, site contact's or rep's name, phone, email or home address into your notes or the file.

For each order, note privately:

- **The order:** where it was made (job software, accounting software, Word, an email), its number
  and how it's built, the job reference, cost code if any, delivery address and instructions,
  required-by date, customer's order number if quoted, each line (supplier code, description,
  quantity, unit, price), freight, GST per line or at the bottom, totals, any note to the supplier.
- **The supplier quote:** which lines made it onto the order, which were changed or dropped, whether
  the order says "as per quote" instead of listing lines, whether the quote looks short against the
  job, and how long it's valid.
- **The docket:** signed or not, order number on it or not, anything short, damaged or substituted.
- **The bill:** every difference from the order (price, quantity, extras such as freight, cut fees,
  small-order fees, levies), and whether it covers more than one order or job.
- **Price lists:** supplier, date, units and pack sizes, any trade discount.
- **An order template or takeoff:** the fields it has, and where the quantities on the order came from.

Start the running step list from what the files show (who made the order, in what, when it was
sent, when the bill came), and mark each step `from file` until they confirm it.

Then say what you worked out in four or five lines and ask **"Have I got that right?"** Never ask a
question that a file already answers; say "got that from your order".
