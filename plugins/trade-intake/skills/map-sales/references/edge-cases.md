# The quick-fire sweep: the odd ones in new work

Run this in Part 6 (Q27 to Q30), after the normal way is down and the step-by-step "is it always
like this?" (Q26) is done. Its job is to catch the cases a tool gets wrong because nobody thought
to mention them: the enquiry that isn't what it looks like, the job that goes quiet, the yes that
arrives in the wrong form, the crew that never hears what was promised.

## How to run it

- **Four rounds, one message each.** Each round counts as **one** question toward the 35, however
  many lines it has.
- **Drop any line already covered** by their files or earlier answers. If a whole round is
  covered, skip it, say so in one line, and don't count it.
- Show the round as a short numbered list in plain words, then ask: **"Which of these happen to
  you? Just say the numbers, and a word on what you do."** Voice answers like "two, we just tarp
  it; five, all the time" are perfect.
- Lines they don't pick are recorded as `doesn't happen`. Lines they pick get **one line** each in
  the file. Only if a pick opens a new rule (who to say no to, when to chase, what counts as a yes),
  ask **one** short ↳ follow-up (it counts).
- If you're close to 35, run rounds 1 and 2 and mark the rest `not asked (question limit)` with the
  defaults below marked `(assumed)`.
- Don't explain the lines. If they ask "what do you mean?", give the Banksia example.
- "Don't know" goes on the parking list with who could answer it.
- Read any rule back: "So: storm-night enquiries get a tarp first and a quote later. Right?"
- Start the round with its name, e.g. `Odd ones, round 2 of 4 · bad info, changes and timing`.

Examples in italics are from the made-up business, Banksia Roofing.

## Round 1 of 4 · enquiries that aren't the usual (Q27)

1. **Size:** tiny jobs or big jobs handled differently (who rings back, whether you visit)?
   _(Gutter cleans: price over the phone. Re-roofs: I go myself, same week.)_
2. **Who it's for:** homeowner vs builder vs property manager or strata vs a business: a different
   way of doing it, not just a different price? _(Property managers want three photos and a price
   by email, no chat.)_
3. **Insurance work:** the assessor's scope, a claim number, the insurer's approval before you
   start, an excess the homeowner pays? _(Assessor sends the scope; we wait for approval; the
   homeowner pays the excess to us.)_
4. **Emergencies and rush:** storm nights, active leaks, "need it this week". Different reply,
   call-out fee, skip the quote? _(Storms: tarp first at the call-out rate, quote the repair later.)_
5. **Repeat customers or the same house again:** do they skip the queue or the visit? _(Regular
   builder: no visit, I price from the plans the same week.)_
6. **Same job to several builders:** one copy each, same price, their name on it? _(Two builders
   on the same house: two quotes, same price.)_
7. **Same customer through different people:** an agent, assessor, strata manager or head
   contractor asks for someone else. Who's the customer, who's on site, who gets the invoice?
   _(Manager asks, tenant's on site, owner pays.)_
8. **Sites with rules:** tenanted, heights, asbestos, schools, inductions, pets, wet weather.
   Who arranges access, how far ahead? _(Tenanted: the manager books with the tenant, a week out.
   Pre-1990: asbestos check before I go up.)_
9. **Lead sites and paid leads:** the same lead sent to several trades; reply in minutes or lose
   it? _(Lead site: first to ring usually wins, so the office rings straight away.)_
10. **A small question that's really a big job,** or a job worth doing cheap for what it leads to
    (a new builder, a strata manager with 40 buildings)? _(A "gutter clean" was a strata block.
    I'll do a cheap first job for a new property manager.)_
11. **Flat out:** when you're busiest, what gets dropped? _(Storm season: small repairs don't get
    quoted and nobody chases.)_
12. **Half an enquiry:** no address, no photos, "just ballpark it", tyre kickers? _(I give a range
    by phone and don't write it down.)_

Default for the tool: flag the kind of enquiry it thinks this is and ask the owner when unsure;
never treat the person asking as the payer without checking.

## Round 2 of 4 · bad info, changes and timing (Q28)

1. **Missing details:** no address, no photos, wrong number, can't reach them. How many tries?
   _(Two texts, then it drops off.)_
2. **Wrong info:** "small leak" is a whole roof; "single storey" is a split-level; the photos are of
   the neighbour's house. _(I always ask the roof's age and the storeys.)_
3. **The same enquiry twice:** a form and a phone call from the same person, or two people from one
   house. _(Happens; we've booked two visits for one house.)_
4. **No-shows and rebooks:** nobody home, weather, the crew ran over. Who rebooks and how?
   _(Partner rebooks by text; wet days push everything a day.)_
5. **Scope changes after the quote:** revised quote, an option added, "can you just do the front"?
   Same number with a version, or a new one? How fast must it go back? _(Same number, rev A,
   within a couple of days.)_
6. **Out of stock or price moved:** a colour or sheet discontinued, a supplier price expired after
   you quoted? _(Swap to the nearest colour and tell them; re-check sheet prices over 30 days.)_
7. **Accepted late:** they say yes after the quote's expired, or months later. Same price? _(Over
   30 days I re-check the sheet price first.)_
8. **Gone quiet:** how many chases, how far apart, when do you give up? _(Text after a week, then
   forget.)_
9. **On hold:** selling the house, waiting on insurance, "next year". How do you come back to it?
   _(I don't.)_
10. **Deadlines:** a builder's tender closing date, an insurer's deadline? _(Builders want it in a
    week; insurers within five working days of the visit.)_
11. **After hours and weekends:** enquiries at night, on Sunday; do you reply then? _(Form comes in
    at night; nobody sees it until morning.)_

Default for the tool: chase missing details twice, then ask the owner; every quiet or on-hold job
gets a next date; never mark anything dead without asking.

## Round 3 of 4 · money and decisions (Q29)

1. **Written down or in your head:** which jobs to take, which builders to avoid, your chances,
   how hard to chase? _(All in my head.)_
2. **Who can say yes to a price or a job:** any dollar amount where it needs your sign-off? Who
   covers when you're away? _(Only me. When I'm away, enquiries wait.)_
3. **Deposits:** how much, on which jobs, inc or ex GST, before or after you order? _(20% inc GST
   on re-roofs, before we order sheets.)_
4. **Progress claims and retention:** builder jobs paid in stages, money held back? _(Builders pay
   monthly; one holds 5%.)_
5. **Inc or ex GST mix-ups:** customers comparing your inc GST total with someone's ex GST price?
   _(Builders want ex GST; homeowners want inc.)_
6. **Who you invoice:** the right business name and ABN; trusts, head contractors, the insurer not
   the assessor? _(Invoiced the assessor once.)_
7. **Discounts and price matching:** do you ever drop the price to win? Who decides? _(Never under
   my minimum; I decide.)_
8. **Supplier price holds:** how long does a supplier price last, and does that limit your quote?
   _(Sheet prices held 30 days, same as my quote.)_
9. **The yes in the wrong form:** a text, a phone call, a PO that arrives weeks later, an approval
   email from someone else? _(Regular builder texts "go"; the PO comes after we've started.)_

Default for the tool: show inc and ex GST on every amount; ask for the business name and ABN at the
win; materials wait for a deposit or PO from new customers.

## Round 4 of 4 · people, records and when things break (Q30)

1. **If you're off for two weeks:** who answers enquiries, books visits, prices? What only you
   know? _(Partner books; nobody prices. Which builders pay late is only in my head.)_
2. **Typing it twice:** every place a detail gets typed again (text → diary → Tradify → Xero →
   the crew's text). _(Enquiry from my texts into Tradify; quote total into Xero; handover retyped
   from the quote.)_
3. **Messy records:** the same customer saved twice, jobs named differently in different places,
   two lists that disagree. Which one is right? _(Tradify for quotes; nothing for enquiries.)_
4. **Notes and promises:** call notes, things promised on site, a call about three jobs at once.
   Where do they go, and has one ever caused an argument? _(Promised a colour match on the ridge
   capping; the crew didn't know.)_
5. **When it breaks:** phone lost or flat, voicemail full, form emails in spam, a quote sent to the
   wrong person. What then? _(Website form went to spam for a fortnight once.)_
6. **Done means:** when a job is won, lost or finished, who's told and how? Does anyone ask why it
   was lost, or for a review? _(Win: text the leading hand. Lost: no one. Reviews: sometimes.)_
7. **After handover:** does the customer still ring you instead of the crew? _(Always.)_
8. **What you can't see:** anything you'd want counted but never is (enquiries turned away,
   jobs you couldn't fit in, what people asked for that you don't do)? _(Never kept a count of
   the ones I said no to.)_

Default for the tool: one list as the truth; every won job gets a handover with what was promised;
lost reasons recorded; the customer is told in writing who their job contact is.

## Where the answers go

In the file's **Exceptions and edge cases** table (see `intake-file.md`): one row per line they
picked, with the step it touches, what they do now, any rule it creates (R number), and where it
came from (`sweep 1` to `sweep 4`, `Q26`, `test case`). Lines not picked are summed up in one
sentence ("Didn't apply: …"). Anything that creates a check for the tool also goes under **What
they want the tool to do** as a check, and each exception that changes a step gets a red marker on
the drawing.
