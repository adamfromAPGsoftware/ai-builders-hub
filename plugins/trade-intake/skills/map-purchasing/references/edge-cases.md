# The quick-fire sweep: the odd ones in ordering

Run this in Part 6 (Q25 to Q28), after the normal way is down and the step-by-step "is it always
like this?" (Q24) is done. Its job is to catch the cases a tool gets wrong because nobody thought to
mention them.

## How to run it

- **Four rounds, one message each.** Each round counts as **one** question toward the 35, however
  many lines it has.
- **Drop any line already covered** by their orders, bills or earlier answers. If a whole round is
  covered, skip it and say so in one line.
- Show the round as a short numbered list in plain words, then ask: **"Which of these happen to
  you? Just say the numbers, and a word on what you do."** Voice answers like "three, all the time,
  I just ring the rep; seven, never" are perfect.
- Lines they don't pick are recorded as `doesn't happen`. Lines they pick get **one line** each in
  the file. Only if a pick opens a new rule (a threshold, a check, a block), ask **one** short ↳
  follow-up (it counts).
- Don't explain the lines. If they ask "what do you mean?", give the Ironbark example.
- "Don't know" goes on the parking list with who could answer it.
- Start the round with its name, e.g. `Odd ones, round 2 of 4 · bad info, changes and timing`.

Examples in italics are from the made-up business, Ironbark Plumbing.

## Round 1 of 4 · orders that aren't the usual (Q25)

1. **Size:** tiny top-ups or big orders handled differently? _(Under $500 the leading hand rings the
   counter; over $3,000 I see it first.)_
2. **Who it's for:** house vs builder vs commercial: a different process, not just a different
   price? Builder's own order number on everything? _(Builders send an order number; it goes on my
   invoice, not the supplier order.)_
3. **Insurance or strata work:** the insurer's scope, their approved suppliers, or proof of what
   you bought? _(Don't do insurance.)_
4. **Emergencies and rush jobs:** burst pipe, hot water out today. Is there an order at all, or a
   counter buy on card? _(Counter on card, no order, receipt in the ute.)_
5. **Repeat customers or the same job again:** do you copy the last order? _(Same builder's next
   townhouse, I copy the last order and change the job number.)_
6. **Variations after the fact:** extra work mid-job adds material. New order, new code, or tacked
   onto the old one? _(New letter on the job number, coded as a variation, if I remember.)_
7. **Specials and made-to-measure:** custom vanity, special-order tapware, cut-to-length. How is it
   described, and can it be returned? _(Described with the rep's quote number; no returns on specials.)_
8. **Mixed orders:** several jobs on one order, or one job split across two suppliers? _(Never two
   jobs on one order; often two suppliers for one job.)_
9. **Not bought new:** from the van or yard, leftovers from another job, or the customer or builder
   supplies it? How do those lines stay off the order and the cost? _(Customer supplies their own
   tapware about one job in five.)_
10. **Ordered before the order exists:** the crew ring the rep or grab it at the counter, and you
    find out later? _(Small fittings, all the time; I find out when the bill comes.)_
11. **Flat out:** in the busiest weeks, what gets dropped? _(Bills don't get checked; I pay them.)_

## Round 2 of 4 · bad info, changes and timing (Q26)

1. **No list to order from:** no plans, no takeoff, "just get what you need"? _(Service jobs: I
   order off the photo the plumber sends.)_
2. **Wrong quantities:** the takeoff or quote was short or long, found out on site? _(Pipe runs
   longer than the plans; second order same week.)_
3. **Supplier quote short or wrong:** a missed line, wrong size, wrong spec? _(Missed lines are
   common; I check line by line.)_
4. **Old prices:** the price list or supplier quote is out of date, or prices went up between the
   quote and the bill? Who notices? _(I notice on the bill, if I notice.)_
5. **Supplier quote expired:** the job got pushed back and the quote's no longer good? _(I ring for
   a new price if it's over 30 days.)_
6. **Out of stock or discontinued:** who picks the swap, and does the customer need to OK it? _(If
   the customer chose it, they pick; otherwise I do.)_
7. **Changing a sent order:** add, remove or swap a line after it's gone. Same order number with a
   note, or a new one? Who tells the supplier? _(Same number, I email the rep "amended".)_
8. **Cancelled order:** job falls over or gets pushed months. Restocking fee? _(Happened twice; 15%
   restocking on one.)_
9. **Back orders and part deliveries:** what stays open, who chases, how often? _(Nobody chases
   until site says it's missing.)_
10. **Delivery timing:** site not open, early starts, weekends, deliveries to the wrong site?
    _(Before 7am only; one went to the wrong site once.)_
11. **Not quite what you ordered, for a normal reason:** coils come a few metres over, packs round
    up, a different brand of the same thing? _(Coils over is fine.)_

## Round 3 of 4 · money and decisions (Q27)

1. **Written down or in your head:** which supplier, how much spare, whether to order now or wait?
   _(All in my head.)_
2. **Who can say yes:** any dollar amount that needs your OK? Who covers when you're away? _(Only
   me over $3,000; when I'm away, big orders wait.)_
3. **Inc or ex GST mix-ups:** order ex, bill inc, price list one way, counter receipt the other?
   _(Price list ex, counter receipt inc. I get confused.)_
4. **Freight and extras:** delivery fees, cut fees, pallet charges, small-order fees, fuel levy.
   Expected or a surprise? Split across jobs? _(Freight's a surprise; never split.)_
5. **Paying up front:** deposits to the supplier, pay before dispatch, cash-on-delivery suppliers?
   _(Special-order vanities: 50% up front.)_
6. **Supplier terms and statements:** different terms per supplier; do you check the monthly
   statement against the bills? _(30 days end of month; statements go straight in the bin.)_
7. **Credits and returns:** who chases them, how do you know they came through, any restocking
   fee? _(I keep it in my head; some never turn up.)_
8. **One bill, several orders or jobs:** or one order billed in bits? _(Supplier A bills each
   delivery separately.)_
9. **Small differences:** cents out from rounding, a price a few dollars up. Pay or query?
   _(Under $20 I just pay.)_
10. **Progress claims and retention:** on builder jobs, do material costs need to line up with
    what you claim each month? _(Not yet.)_

## Round 4 of 4 · people, records and when things break (Q28)

1. **If you're off for two weeks:** who orders, who checks the bills? What only you know (which
   rep, which supplier for what, the cost codes)? _(Nobody. Only I know the codes.)_
2. **Typing it twice:** every place a number gets typed again (quote → order → bill check →
   Xero). _(Quote lines into Fergus; bill total checked against Fergus by eye.)_
3. **Messy records:** the same supplier set up twice in Xero, the same item called three things
   (crew name, supplier code, bill description), two copies of a price list. Which one is right?
   _(Supplier A is in Xero twice; I use the newer one.)_
4. **Where the truth lives:** if the order in the software, the email and the bill disagree,
   which wins? _(The email, because that's what the rep saw.)_
5. **Lost paperwork:** dockets lost, bills never arrived, a supplier says they never got the
   order? _(Dockets lost all the time.)_
6. **Paid twice:** card at the counter and the emailed bill paid as well, or a bill entered twice?
   _(Once that I know of.)_
7. **When it breaks:** bill-capture app stops syncing, job software down, the order email bounces?
   _(Fergus went down for a day; ordered by phone.)_
8. **Did the job's materials come in on budget:** does anyone compare what was bought with what
   was quoted? _(Only when a job feels light.)_
9. **Done means:** when the gear arrives or the last bill's paid, who's told and how? _(Crew find
   out when it's on site; customer isn't told.)_

## Where the answers go

In the file's **Exceptions and edge cases** table (see `intake-file.md`): one row per line they
picked, with the step it touches, what they do now, any rule or check it creates, and where it came
from (`sweep 1`, `Q24`, `test case`). Lines not picked are summed up in one sentence ("Didn't apply:
…"). Anything that creates a check for the tool also goes under **What they want the tool to do** as
a check, and each exception that changes a step gets a red marker on the drawing.
